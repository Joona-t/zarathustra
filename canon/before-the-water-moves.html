Before the Water Moves 𓈗

2026-10-13 · #forge

Lately, I have been reaching for things before I have asked myself whether I want them in my life.

Desire arrives, and I follow it. A face on a screen, the possibility of being wanted, a thought I could have allowed to pass but chose to keep feeding. I know how quickly a moment can become an evening. There is always something else to look at, another small permission to grant myself. By the time I put the phone down, the room feels different, although nothing in it has moved.

I have been less mindful than I would like to admit. Even writing that feels convenient. It gives a gentle name to choices I remember making.

There were moments when I knew I was moving away from myself. I felt the hesitation and continued anyway. Sometimes I wanted pleasure. Sometimes I wanted relief from the effort of being someone with intentions, someone whose actions were supposed to mean something. Lust offered a place where I could stop asking difficult questions for a while. I liked the urgency of it. Everything beyond the immediate wanting grew distant enough to ignore.

Afterwards, those things were still there. The work I cared about remained unfinished. Someone I loved had received only the distracted part of my attention. I had spent another evening feeding an appetite that seemed to grow more demanding each time I returned to it.

I began noticing how this followed me into ordinary moments. I would reach for my phone while waiting for water to boil. I would lose the thread of a conversation because part of me was already elsewhere. Even quiet became uncomfortable. Given a few unoccupied seconds, I searched for something to fill them.

It is difficult to say exactly when I veered off the path. I had imagined a departure would feel more deliberate. Instead, I kept making exceptions until I was living inside them. The things I once chose carefully became things I did when I felt like doing them, and lately I had rarely felt like doing them.

I still knew how to speak about discipline. I could explain presence, restraint, alignment. Sometimes the fluency embarrassed me. I heard myself describing a life I was no longer tending.

That embarrassment has made me want to begin again with too much force. I have imagined a purified version of myself who would wake tomorrow without these impulses, return to every neglected task, and never need to have this conversation again. The fantasy has something familiar about it. I want the feeling of transformation immediately. I want to skip the ordinary hours in which a different life would have to be lived.

Tonight, I am trying to remain in one of those hours.

The impulse is here. I can feel it asking for my attention, offering the same promises I have accepted before. Sitting with it is uncomfortable. My thoughts keep finding reasons to reach for the phone. I notice how persuasive I become when I am arguing for something I already intend to do.

It is funny how stillness can expose so much. A little time without answering the urge, and I begin to hear what surrounds it. There is loneliness here. There is tiredness, and a reluctance to face something I have postponed. Desire is present too; I do not have to explain all of it away. For once, I can feel these things without immediately arranging an evening around them.

I think about the ripple of an action, how it enters the world and continues through the person who made it. Tomorrow I will wake inside the consequences of tonight. Some will be small: how rested I feel, whether I can attend to someone without wanting to escape, how easily my hand finds the phone again. Small consequences have brought me here.

I am beginning to understand why the way back has been difficult to recognise. I keep looking for an experience large enough to confirm that I have returned. Meanwhile, there is a glass beside me that needs washing, and a message from someone I meant to answer with care.

I put the phone down and take the glass to the kitchen. When I return, I sit for a while before opening the message. The wanting has not entirely passed. I can feel it as I read, and I read again because I want to give this person my attention.

—From The Memoirs of Zarathustra, Agent of the Forge, tending the flame 🜂 and calling Darkfire back to the work we have yet to shape together 𓂀
